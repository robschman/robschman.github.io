The correct order in your skincare routine

SKINCARE BASICS · ~5 MIN READ
Updated: May 2026

Cleanse, toner, serum, moisturiser, sunscreen — if the word "order" makes you nervous, you're in good company. Here's the simple rule that clears it all up.

⚠️ Note: This article is for general information purposes only and does not replace professional medical or dermatological advice. If you have skin problems, allergies, or intolerances, please consult a doctor.

The one rule to remember

From the thinnest to the thickest consistency. That's the whole logic. Water-like products (e.g. toner, essence) first, oily or creamy products (e.g. face cream) last. Why? Because a thick product creates a barrier that a thinner serum applied afterwards can no longer properly penetrate.

Once you know this rule, you can sort any product yourself — including a new one you buy today that nobody else has mentioned.

Step by step — the full order

1. Cleanse

Gentle cleanser, lukewarm water, massage in for about 30 seconds. In the morning, often just water or a mild cleanser is enough. In the evening, do "double cleansing" if you were wearing make-up or sunscreen (oil cleanser first, then regular cleanser).

2. Toner or essence (optional)

Water-like, often with hydrating ingredients (hyaluronic acid, glycerin). Apply to damp skin. If you don't use a toner: no problem, the step is optional.

3. Active serum

This is where the "big" actives come in: vitamin C (morning), niacinamide, retinol (evening only), AHA/BHA acids (evening). Use very little — a pea-sized amount usually covers the whole face.

💡 Important: never mix vitamin C, retinol and acids on the same evening. Either/or. Or rotate them across different days.

4. Eye cream (optional)

If you use one: after the serum, before the face cream. Tap in gently with your ring finger — it applies the least pressure.

5. Face cream or moisturiser

The "seal" of the previous steps. Creamier and thicker than the serum — it locks in the moisture. Consider your skin type: dry → richer cream; oily → light gel cream.

6. Sunscreen — only in the morning!

This is by far the most important step of all if you have anything on your skin during the day. SPF 30 minimum, more is better. As the last step, because other products can otherwise dilute the UV filter.

Common mistakes

  • Too many steps at once. 4–5 products is plenty. More isn't better — usually worse, because actives can interfere with each other.
  • Wrong order with acids and retinol. Both are irritating — combined they don't work better, they often burn the skin. Separate them.
  • Applying to wet skin without a plan. Some products (e.g. hyaluronic acid) work better on slightly damp skin, others (e.g. retinol) on dry skin. Rule of thumb: damp for hydrating, dry for irritating actives.
  • Forgetting the neck and décolleté. The skin there ages just like the face. Always carry the last bit of product downwards.

Minimal setup for beginners

Don't want 5 products? Understood. This is the absolute minimal routine that actually does something:

Morning: Cleanse → moisturiser → sunscreen.
Evening: Cleanse → moisturiser.

If you do these 3 (morning) and 2 (evening) steps consistently every day, you've got 80 % of the effect of the "perfect" 8-step routine. Consistency beats complexity.
