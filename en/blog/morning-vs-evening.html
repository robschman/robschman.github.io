Morning vs. evening routine

SKINCARE BASICS · ~6 MIN READ
Updated: May 2026

During the day your skin has to defend itself against UV radiation, pollution and stress. At night it wants to recover and repair. Out of that difference come pretty clear rules about what product belongs when.

⚠️ Note: This article is for general information purposes only and does not replace professional medical or dermatological advice. If you have skin problems, allergies, or intolerances, please consult a doctor.

Morning: protect

The top goal of your morning routine is protection. Protection against UV radiation, against free radicals from pollution, against drying out from heating or air-conditioning. Your morning care should therefore:

  • Supply antioxidants that neutralise free radicals — the classic is vitamin C.
  • Lock in moisture so the skin barrier stays intact — hyaluronic acid, glycerin, niacinamide.
  • Guarantee UV protection — sunscreen with SPF 30 minimum, every day, even in winter.

What you don't use in the morning: retinol, AHA/BHA acids, vitamin A. They actually make your skin more light-sensitive in the short term, which is exactly the opposite of what you want during the day.

Evening: repair

At night your skin is in "repair mode". The skin barrier rebuilds, old cells are shed, collagen is formed. That's exactly when it makes sense to work with more active ingredients that support this process:

  • Retinol (vitamin A derivatives) — boosts cell turnover, smooths fine lines, helps with acne.
  • Acids (AHA like glycolic acid, BHA like salicylic acid) — gently exfoliate, refine the skin's texture.
  • Rich creams or sleeping masks — the seal for the actives.
  • Peptides — considered a gentle alternative to retinol, can be combined with almost anything.

What you don't need in the evening: sunscreen. Obviously — the sun is gone.

💡 Golden rule: when starting retinol, use it only 2–3× per week, and no acids on the same evening. Slowly increase frequency after several weeks if your skin tolerates it.

Both routines compared

Morning (3–5 min):

  1. Cleanse (gently or just water)
  2. Antioxidant serum (e.g. vitamin C)
  3. Moisturiser
  4. Sunscreen

Evening (5–10 min):

  1. Make-up / sunscreen removal (oil cleanser or micellar)
  2. Cleanse (second step: regular cleanser)
  3. Treatment serum (retinol or acid — not both)
  4. Eye cream (optional)
  5. Rich cream or sleeping mask

You don't have to do every step. Just consistently doing sunscreen in the morning and cleansing + moisturiser in the evening already makes the biggest step forward.

Frequent confusions

Vitamin C — morning, evening, or both? Both work, but morning is the classic, because as an antioxidant it provides the most benefit during the day. If you also use it in the evening, split it across two different products to avoid irritating your skin.

Niacinamide — when? Doesn't matter. Niacinamide is very well tolerated and can be used both morning and evening. Also in combination with retinol or acids.

Hyaluronic acid — when? Whenever your skin needs moisture. So: usually both. But especially in the evening, when it has time to work overnight.

Sunscreen at night? No. Waste — the UV filter only works during the day. Unless you're sitting in front of a screen all evening: even then, regular daytime sunscreen is enough, because it also filters some blue light. Otherwise just normal evening care.
